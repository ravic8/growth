# Personalization Model

## Purpose

Growth should not produce the same checklist for every user. Onboarding answers
must become a personalized system with explicit goals, constraints, metrics,
recommendations, and review rules.

## Core User Model

### Identity and Context

- Life stage, location, responsibilities, and relevant background.
- Current roles and important relationships.
- Available time, energy, money, tools, and support.
- Health, accessibility, privacy, and safety constraints.

### Transformation

- Current state.
- Desired state.
- Target date.
- Motivation.
- Definition of success.
- Non-negotiables and unacceptable tradeoffs.

### Capability

- Existing skills, habits, assets, and advantages.
- Gaps, bottlenecks, risks, and dependencies.
- Previous attempts and lessons.
- Confidence and readiness.

### Behavior and Preferences

- Planning style.
- Coaching tone.
- Accountability preferences.
- Sustainable workload.
- Preferred activities and avoided activities.
- Likely failure patterns.

## From Answers to a Plan

Growth should transform onboarding answers in this order:

1. **Select a primary transformation.**
   Choose the most important near-term outcome while preserving non-negotiables.

2. **Establish the baseline.**
   Record the starting state and confidence level. Unknown baselines become early
   discovery tasks.

3. **Define success metrics.**
   Include lagging outcomes, leading indicators, and safeguards against harmful
   tradeoffs.

4. **Identify the highest-leverage bottleneck.**
   The plan should address what currently limits progress, not every possible
   skill or habit.

5. **Respect capacity and constraints.**
   Recommendations must fit the user's real schedule, energy, money, health,
   responsibilities, and risk tolerance.

6. **Create a 30-day strategy.**
   Define the approach, assumptions being tested, milestones, and conditions
   that would cause the strategy to change.

7. **Create a small weekly plan.**
   Commitments should be measurable, achievable, and connected to evidence.

8. **Learn and adapt.**
   Weekly reviews compare planned actions, completed work, external signals,
   outcomes, wellbeing, and user feedback.

## Example: Better Job

User answers:

- Current: data engineer earning X.
- Target: AI platform engineer earning Y within nine months.
- Constraint: full-time role and ten available hours per week.
- Gap: limited system-design demonstration and weak recruiter response rate.
- Preference: direct coaching and structured weekly plans.

Customized system:

- Primary metric: qualified interview pipeline and eventual offer quality.
- Initial strategy: improve positioning, produce two strong architecture stories,
  and run a targeted application and referral experiment.
- Weekly plan: a small number of interview demonstrations, tailored applications,
  and conversations.
- Adaptation: change positioning or channels if response rate remains weak;
  change preparation focus when interview feedback reveals repeated gaps.

## Example: Become Fit

User answers:

- Current: inconsistent exercise, low energy, and limited equipment.
- Target: improve strength, energy, and body composition.
- Constraint: three 40-minute sessions per week and a previous knee injury.
- Preference: gradual progress without calorie obsession.

Customized system:

- Primary metrics: training consistency, strength progression, energy, and agreed
  body-composition indicator.
- Safeguards: injury-aware plan, recovery, and no obsessive tracking.
- Weekly plan: three appropriate sessions plus simple nutrition and sleep actions.
- Adaptation: adjust volume based on recovery, adherence, and professional
  guidance.

## Recommendation Requirements

Every recommendation should be able to answer:

1. Which user goal does this support?
2. Why is it important now?
3. Which evidence or user answer caused this recommendation?
4. How much time, energy, money, or risk does it require?
5. What would successful completion look like?
6. When should the recommendation be changed or stopped?

## Learning Without Losing Trust

- Treat inferred preferences as hypotheses, not facts.
- Clearly distinguish user-provided facts from system inferences.
- Ask before using sensitive data.
- Explain significant plan changes.
- Let users correct, delete, export, or disable learned information.
- Never optimize engagement at the expense of the user's stated wellbeing.
