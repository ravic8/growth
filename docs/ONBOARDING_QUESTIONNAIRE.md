# Growth Personalization Onboarding

## Purpose

This conversation helps Growth understand the user well enough to create a
realistic, personalized improvement system. It should feel like a thoughtful
coach getting to know someone, not like completing a tax form.

The product should:

- Explain why important questions are being asked.
- Ask one small group of questions at a time.
- Allow users to skip sensitive or unknown answers.
- Offer examples when a question may feel abstract.
- Ask follow-up questions only when relevant.
- Summarize its understanding and let the user correct it.
- Never create a plan until the user approves the final summary.

## Suggested Conversation Flow

Use five short onboarding stages:

1. **Your Direction:** what the user wants to change and why.
2. **Your Starting Point:** current reality and previous attempts.
3. **Your Life:** time, energy, resources, preferences, and constraints.
4. **Relevant Deep Dive:** detailed questions only for selected goal areas.
5. **Your Growth System:** coaching style, tracking, privacy, and plan approval.

The first session should collect enough information to create a useful initial
plan. Remaining details can be learned gradually during weekly reviews.

---

## Stage 1: Your Direction

Intro shown to the user:

> Let's begin with what you want life to look like. You do not need perfect
> answers. We will make the goals clearer together.

1. What would you most like to improve in your life right now?

   Example areas: health, career, income, business, learning, relationships,
   finances, creativity, confidence, or lifestyle.

2. If we could make meaningful progress in only one area during the next 90
   days, which area should it be?

3. What other areas matter, even if they receive less attention for now?

4. What specifically would you like to be different?

   Examples: "move from a data engineer role to an AI platform role," "reduce
   weight from 90 kg to 80 kg," or "launch my first paid product."

5. Where are you today, and where would you like to reach?

6. When would you ideally like to achieve this outcome? Is that date flexible?

7. Why does this change matter to you personally?

8. What would achieving it make possible?

9. What might happen if nothing changes during the next year?

10. How important is this goal from 1-10?

11. How confident are you that you can achieve it from 1-10?

12. What would make your confidence one point higher?

13. How will you know the goal has genuinely been achieved?

14. Are there any outcomes you do **not** want to sacrifice while pursuing it?

15. Who else is affected by this goal or should be involved in it?

---

## Stage 2: Your Starting Point

Intro shown to the user:

> A useful plan starts from your real life, not an ideal version of it. These
> questions help us avoid plans that look impressive but do not survive Monday.

1. What is already going well in this area?

2. What are the biggest problems or gaps today?

3. What have you tried before?

4. What worked, even temporarily?

5. What did not work, and why do you think it failed?

6. What usually causes you to stop or lose momentum?

7. Which parts feel easy, enjoyable, or natural to you?

8. Which parts do you avoid, procrastinate on, or find intimidating?

9. What skills, habits, resources, or relationships do you already have that can
   help?

10. What do you believe is the biggest bottleneck?

11. Has anyone given you feedback about this goal or your current approach?

12. Are there assumptions you are making that we should test?

13. What evidence or progress have you already produced?

14. How do you currently plan and track progress?

15. Which tools, apps, documents, or systems do you already use?

---

## Stage 3: Your Life and Constraints

Intro shown to the user:

> The right plan is one you can sustain alongside the rest of your life. Growth
> will use these answers to choose the right pace and protect what matters.

### Time and Schedule

1. Describe a typical weekday and weekend.

2. How many hours per week can you realistically invest in this goal?

3. When do you usually have the most energy and focus?

4. Which times are generally available for focused work, exercise, or learning?

5. Which commitments are fixed and non-negotiable?

6. Are there upcoming events, deadlines, travel, or life changes that may affect
   the plan?

### Energy and Wellbeing

7. How would you describe your current energy, sleep, stress, and overall health?

8. Are there health conditions, injuries, accessibility needs, or professional
   recommendations the plan must respect?

9. What helps you recover when work or life becomes demanding?

10. What are the early signs that you are overloaded or burning out?

### Resources and Environment

11. What budget can you comfortably invest in this goal?

12. What equipment, technology, learning resources, or professional support do
    you have access to?

13. Is your home or work environment supportive of this goal?

14. Who can support, encourage, mentor, or hold you accountable?

15. Are there people or circumstances that may make progress harder?

### Working Preferences

16. Do you prefer a structured plan or flexible recommendations?

17. Do you work better with small daily actions, longer focused sessions, or a
    combination?

18. Do you prefer doing one major goal at a time or balancing several?

19. How much novelty versus routine helps you stay engaged?

20. What kind of reminders are helpful, and which become annoying?

---

## Stage 4: Relevant Goal Deep Dives

Ask only the sections related to the user's selected goals.

### Career and Better Job

1. What is your current role, title, company type, industry, and seniority?

2. What do you currently earn? Include base, bonus, equity, currency, and any
   benefits that matter. The user may skip this.

3. What target role, seniority, company type, industry, and compensation range do
   you want?

4. Are there specific companies, founders, industries, or types of work you
   prefer?

5. Are there companies, industries, cultures, or responsibilities you want to
   avoid?

6. Why do you want to leave or change your current situation?

7. Which matters most: compensation, learning, impact, title, stability,
   flexibility, location, mission, ownership, or work-life balance?

8. Rank the previous factors in priority order.

9. What locations, time zones, remote arrangements, and travel requirements are
   acceptable?

10. Do you need visa sponsorship or have employment constraints?

11. What is your notice period and earliest possible start date?

12. How many years of relevant experience do you have?

13. What are your strongest technical, business, leadership, and communication
    skills?

14. What work achievements best demonstrate your value? Include scale, impact,
    ownership, and measurable results where possible.

15. Which skills or experiences are missing for your target role?

16. How comfortable are you with resume defense, coding interviews, system
    design, behavioral interviews, take-home work, and salary negotiation?

17. What does your current application and interview pipeline look like?

18. How many quality applications, referrals, and conversations can you sustain
    each week?

19. What application channels have worked best for you?

20. Do you have a resume, portfolio, GitHub profile, LinkedIn profile, references,
    or public work ready?

21. What feedback have recruiters or interviewers given you?

22. What would make a new job a bad move even if it paid more?

23. How urgently do you need to change jobs?

24. Would you accept a stepping-stone role, or only the final target role?

### Fitness and Health

Health plans must avoid diagnosis or treatment and should encourage professional
guidance when appropriate.

1. What does "fit and healthy" mean to you personally?

2. What are your main goals: strength, muscle, fat loss, endurance, mobility,
   energy, sleep, stress, general health, or another outcome?

3. Which goal is most important during the next 90 days?

4. What are your current height, weight, activity level, and relevant baseline
   measurements? All are optional.

5. Are there medical conditions, injuries, medications, pregnancy considerations,
   or clinician instructions the plan must respect?

6. What is your current exercise routine?

7. Which forms of exercise do you enjoy, tolerate, or dislike?

8. What facilities and equipment can you access?

9. How many days and minutes per session can you realistically exercise?

10. What is your training experience and confidence level?

11. Describe your usual meals, protein intake, fruits and vegetables, hydration,
    alcohol, nicotine, and eating patterns.

12. Do you follow dietary preferences or restrictions?

13. How many hours do you sleep, and how consistent is your sleep schedule?

14. What typically disrupts your exercise, nutrition, or recovery?

15. How would you like to measure progress without becoming obsessive?

16. Are there habits you want to reduce or stop?

17. What support from a coach, doctor, trainer, partner, or friend is available?

### Learning and Skill Development

1. What skill do you want to develop, and why?

2. What should you be able to do with the skill when successful?

3. What is your current level, and how do you know?

4. Is there a deadline, exam, interview, project, or real-world use case?

5. How do you learn best: reading, videos, instruction, projects, discussion,
   practice, or teaching?

6. What resources or courses are you already using?

7. What projects or demonstrations could prove your ability?

8. Who can review your work or provide feedback?

9. What topics or situations repeatedly confuse you?

10. How much deliberate practice can you sustain each week?

### Product, Business, or Side Project

1. What are you building, for whom, and what problem does it solve?

2. Why is this problem worth solving?

3. What stage is the product currently in?

4. Who is the ideal user or customer?

5. What evidence shows that users care about the problem?

6. What alternatives or competitors do users have?

7. What is meaningfully different about your approach?

8. What is the smallest useful product you could deliver?

9. What are the biggest product, technical, distribution, or business risks?

10. Which assumptions need testing first?

11. How will you reach and learn from users?

12. How could the product eventually create value or revenue?

13. What skills, collaborators, budget, and technology are available?

14. What constraints or dependencies could slow progress?

15. What would success look like in 30 days, 90 days, and one year?

16. When should you continue, change direction, or stop?

### Money and Financial Improvement

1. What financial outcome do you want and why?

2. What is the timeframe?

3. What are your current income sources, essential expenses, debts, savings, and
   investments? Exact amounts are optional.

4. Which matters most now: income growth, emergency savings, debt reduction,
   spending control, investing, insurance, or another priority?

5. What financial risks or obligations must the plan respect?

6. How predictable are your income and expenses?

7. What habits or decisions currently help or hurt your finances?

8. How comfortable are you with financial risk?

9. Do you work with a qualified financial professional?

10. What financial data are you comfortable tracking in Growth?

### Relationships, Community, and Network

1. Which relationships or communities would you like to strengthen?

2. What does a healthy or useful relationship in this area look like?

3. What is currently missing: time, trust, communication, shared experiences,
   support, visibility, or access?

4. Who are the important people or groups involved?

5. How often would you ideally connect?

6. What kinds of interaction feel genuine and energizing?

7. What boundaries should be protected?

8. Are there difficult conversations or unresolved issues affecting progress?

9. How comfortable are you with outreach and asking for help?

10. What value or support can you offer others?

### Custom Goal

1. Describe the desired transformation in your own words.

2. What is the current baseline?

3. What observable outcome would demonstrate success?

4. Which activities are likely to influence that outcome?

5. Which constraints, risks, and tradeoffs matter?

6. Who can provide feedback or confirm progress?

7. What has worked or failed before?

8. What would a sustainable weekly commitment look like?

---

## Stage 5: Your Growth System

Intro shown to the user:

> We now understand the destination and starting point. These final choices help
> Growth support you in a way that feels useful rather than intrusive.

### Planning and Prioritization

1. Should Growth propose plans for your approval, build plans with you, or let
   you create plans manually?

2. How many active goals feel manageable at once?

3. Should Growth recommend which goals to pause when capacity is limited?

4. Should plans automatically adapt when deadlines, health, workload, or results
   change?

5. What decisions should Growth never make without asking you?

6. What should Growth do when a new idea or opportunity appears?

### Coaching and Accountability

7. What kind of support helps you most: encouragement, direct challenge,
   analysis, questions, reminders, competition, or reflection?

8. How direct should Growth be when it detects avoidance or misleading progress?

9. What should happen after you miss a commitment?

10. Should Growth distinguish between a bad plan, low capacity, unexpected life
    events, and avoidable inconsistency?

11. Who, if anyone, may see your goals or progress?

12. Would accountability with a friend, coach, partner, or group help?

### Tracking and Reviews

13. What are you realistically willing to record daily?

14. Which data should be captured automatically where possible?

15. How much time are you willing to spend on a daily check-in?

16. How much time are you willing to spend on a weekly review?

17. Which progress indicators would motivate you?

18. Which scores, streaks, comparisons, or notifications might create unhealthy
    pressure?

19. How should Growth celebrate progress?

20. How often should long-term goals and priorities be reconsidered?

### Integrations

21. Which tools should Growth eventually connect to?

   Examples: calendar, email, fitness apps, wearable devices, GitHub, LinkedIn,
   job trackers, notes, task managers, financial tools, or product analytics.

22. Which information should Growth import automatically?

23. Which actions should Growth be allowed to take in connected tools?

24. Which integrations are essential, useful later, or unwanted?

### Privacy and Trust

25. Which information is sensitive or should remain only on your device?

26. Which information may be stored in the cloud?

27. Which information may be used to personalize AI recommendations?

28. What should Growth forget automatically?

29. Should Growth provide a complete explanation for every recommendation?

30. How should users export or permanently delete their data?

### Product Experience

31. Where would you prefer to use Growth: mobile, web, desktop, chat, voice, or a
    combination?

32. When are you most likely to interact with it?

33. What should Growth show first when you open it?

34. What would make you stop using the product?

35. Which existing products feel motivating, calm, or well designed to you?

36. Which productivity or coaching products have disappointed you, and why?

---

## Final Confirmation

Growth should summarize:

- The user's desired transformations.
- The current baseline.
- The primary 90-day outcome.
- Important secondary goals.
- Motivation and definition of success.
- Available weekly capacity.
- Constraints, risks, and non-negotiables.
- Preferred planning and coaching style.
- What will and will not be tracked.
- Privacy and integration choices.
- Important unknowns and assumptions.

Then ask:

1. What did Growth understand correctly?
2. What did Growth misunderstand or miss?
3. Does the primary goal still feel right?
4. Does the proposed level of effort feel realistic?
5. Is there anything you are uncomfortable storing or tracking?
6. May Growth create the first 30-day plan?

## Ongoing Discovery

Onboarding is not the final truth. Growth should continue learning through:

- Brief daily check-ins.
- Weekly outcome and capacity reviews.
- Monthly goal and strategy reviews.
- Changes in schedule, health, work, or personal responsibilities.
- Explicit feedback on recommendations.
- Behavioral patterns, with clear user consent.
